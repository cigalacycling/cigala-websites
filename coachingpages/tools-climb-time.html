How it works

HOW WE WORK OUT YOUR CLIMB TIME

On a climb, most of your power goes into lifting you and your bike against gravity, which is why watts per kilo matter so much. The rest goes into rolling resistance and, a little, the air.

We use the standard cycling power equation: gravity, rolling resistance (good road tyres), air resistance for a rider on the hoods, and 2.5% lost in the drivetrain. Air is thinner higher up, so the summit altitude is taken into account.

Why your real time can differ

  • Steeper ramps: the calculation uses the average gradient. Climbs with steep sections and flatter parts usually take a little longer.
  • Pacing: riding evenly is fastest. Going too hard at the bottom costs time at the top.
  • Wind, heat and altitude: above about 2,000 m, most riders can't hold their usual power.
