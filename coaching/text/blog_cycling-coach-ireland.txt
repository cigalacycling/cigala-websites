H2: Why Work With a Cycling Coach?
P: A good cycling coach does more than give you workouts.
P: They provide structure, feedback, and long-term progression.
H3: A coach helps you:
LI: Improve FTP and aerobic capacity
LI: Train consistently without burnout
LI: Peak for key events
LI: Avoid overtraining and injury
LI: Balance training with work and life
LI: Progress year after year (not just short-term gains)
P: Most cyclists don’t fail because they don’t train enough — they fail because they don’t train systematically.
H2: What Makes a Good Cycling Coach in Ireland?
P: Not all coaches are equal. Here are the key factors to consider.
H3: 1. Experience
P: Look for a coach with:
LI: Real racing experience
LI: Coaching experience across different levels
LI: A strong understanding of endurance physiology and long-term athlete development
LI: Understanding & experience of the key cycling events, terrain, and climbing demands within Ireland and internationally, and what is required to perform in these environments.
P: A coach who has worked with both beginners and competitive cyclists is usually more adaptable, as they understand how to scale training appropriately across different abilities and goals.
<img https://coaching.cigalacycling.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/download.png alt='Dan Morrissey Team winning the best County in Ras Tailteann 2026'>
H3: 2. Education and Methodology
P: Good coaching should be based on science, not guesswork.
P: Modern coaching often includes:
LI: Power-based training
LI: Critical Power or FTP analysis
LI: W’ Prime modelling (anaerobic capacity tracking)
LI: Structured training phases and periodisation
LI: Fatigue and recovery management
LI: Strength training to support performance
LI: Mobility work to improve positioning, efficiency, and injury prevention
LI: Basic nutrition support to optimise training adaptation and recovery, with access to an in-house sports nutritionist for 1:1 coaching coordinated through your daily plan.
P: A strong coach should be able to explain why you are doing a workout.
H3: 3. Personalisation
P: Avoid off-the-shelves training plans.
P: A proper coach should adapt training based on:
LI: Your available training time
LI: Stress and fatigue
LI: Work/family life
LI: Equipment (power meter, heart rate, etc.)
LI: Goals and event calendar
P: If your plan never changes, it’s not really coaching.
P: Coaching is a two-way process. A coach provides structure, guidance, and feedback, while the athlete provides consistent data, communication, and honesty about how training is going.
P: This includes:
LI: Daily wellness check-ins
LI: Session feedback
LI: RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)
LI: Training data review
P: Without feedback from both sides, coaching becomes guesswork rather than a structured performance process.
H3: 4. Communication
P: One of the biggest differences between good and great coaching is communication.
P: How quickly are training adjustments made?
P: How often is feedback provided?
P: Is there direct access to the coach when needed (e.g. messaging or scheduled calls)?
P: Clear and consistent communication ensures that training can be adapted in real time when life, fatigue, or performance changes.

H2: Online Cycling Coaching in Ireland
P: Most coaching today is done online — and for good reason.
P: With platforms like **[TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/)** or **[Vekta](https://joinvekta.com/)**, coaches can analyse your training in detail and adjust your plan in real time.
H3: Benefits of online coaching:
LI: Works anywhere in Ireland or abroad
LI: More detailed data analysis than in-person coaching
LI: Flexible communication
LI: Better value than traditional coaching
P: For most cyclists, online coaching is actually more effective than in-person coaching.
H2: Do You Need a Power Meter?
P: Not essential — but highly recommended.
P: A power meter allows your coach to track:
LI: Training load
LI: Fitness trends
LI: Fatigue and recovery
LI: Performance improvements
P: However, heart rate-based coaching can still work well, especially for beginners.
H2: How Much Does a Cycling Coach Cost in Ireland?
P: Prices vary depending on the level of support.
P: Typically:
LI: **Basic Training Plans (Limited Support): €30–€80/month**
Structured training plans designed for self-guided athletes who want direction and consistency, with minimal coach interaction.
LI: **Standard Coaching: €100–€200/month**
Fully personalised coaching with regular plan updates, performance feedback, and ongoing communication.
LI: **High-Performance Coaching: €200–€400+/month**
Comprehensive 1:1 coaching with daily detailed performance analysis, frequent adjustments, race preparation, and close coach-athlete communication.
P: The value of coaching is not defined by price, but by the level of personalisation, feedback, and ongoing support you receive.

H2: Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Coach
P: Before choosing a cycling coach, ask:
LI: Do you personalise training plans?
LI: How often are plans updated?
LI: What platform do you use?
LI: Can training adapt if my schedule changes?
LI: Do you provide race analysis?
LI: How do you measure progress?
LI: How often can we talk?
P: A good coach will answer all of these clearly.
