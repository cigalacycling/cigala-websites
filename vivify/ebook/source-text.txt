Build a balanced breakfast
A useful breakfast combines foods that support your training and help you meet your overall nutritional needs. Think in
terms of four groups:

   Protein-rich foods                                                    Carbohydrate foods

   Eggs, Greek yogurt, skyr, cottage cheese, milk, tofu and              Oats, wholegrain bread, fruit, quinoa, potatoes and
   fish.                                                                 cereals.


   Fruit & vegetables                                                    Healthy fats

   Berries, banana, apples, spinach, tomatoes and peppers.               Nuts, seeds, olive oil, nut butter and avocado.


Protein at breakfast
Including a protein-rich food at breakfast is one practical way to distribute protein across the day. A broad target of around 25–40 g
at a main meal can be useful for many athletes, but needs vary with body size, training load, total daily intake and individual goals.
Not every breakfast needs to hit the same number.
You can build a protein-containing breakfast without supplements: combine dairy foods, eggs, fish or tofu with oats, bread, fruit and
other toppings. If a breakfast is naturally lower in protein, add a side of yogurt, milk or eggs rather than forcing every recipe to be a
high-protein meal.


     TRAINING SOON?
     If you have 1.5–3 hours before training, choose a breakfast that provides carbohydrate and sits comfortably. If you have
     only 30–60 minutes, a smaller, easier-to-digest option may work better. Reduce fibre and fat close to exercise if they cause
     gastrointestinal discomfort. Match portion size to the session and your own tolerance.

A final note on fuelling
There is no single perfect breakfast. The best option is one that you enjoy, tolerate well and can fit around your training,
appetite and schedule. On harder or longer training days, increase the carbohydrate portion or add an extra side. On
lighter days, choose portions that leave you comfortably satisfied.

Whole foods first does not mean supplements are never useful. It simply means these recipes are designed to work
without protein powder, using familiar ingredients that also bring other nutrients, flavour and texture.
