Made for female athletes

Fuel the way your body works

Most sports nutrition advice was built from research on men. Women who ride have their own needs: hormones that change through the month and through life, higher iron needs, and a greater risk of under-fuelling when training is high and food is tight.

Marine races in the women's WorldTour with LIDL-TREK, so she knows these questions from the inside. Your plan is built around your training load, your cycle and your goals, whether that is a first sportive, a gravel race or a season of racing.

The key areas

Four things female cyclists should know

1.Eat enough

Under-fuelling for your training (low energy availability) can affect hormones, bones, mood and performance. Fuel the work first.

2.Watch your iron

Training and menstrual losses raise iron needs. Low iron often shows up as fatigue that rest does not fix.

3.Know your cycle

Energy, appetite and gut comfort can change through the month. Your plan follows how you feel, not a generic chart.

4.Fuel on the bike

Carbohydrate during long rides protects your training and recovery, and that applies to women just as much as men.

Women's cycling nutrition

Common questions

Should I change my nutrition across my menstrual cycle?

Some women notice changes in energy, appetite, hydration or gut comfort at different points of their cycle, and some don’t. The research is still developing, so there is no single plan that suits every woman. Marine looks at your own training, symptoms and food diary and adjusts from there.

Why do female athletes need to think about iron?

Endurance training and menstrual blood loss both raise iron needs, so women who ride a lot are more likely to run low. Low iron can show up as fatigue and a drop in performance. Marine covers this in Iron Deficiency, Anemia and Performance. If you think your iron is low, get your levels checked by your GP.

I want to lose weight but keep my power. Is that possible?

Yes, with care. Eating too little for the training you do (low energy availability) can affect your hormones, bones, recovery and performance. Marine’s approach is fuel for performance, not restriction: enough energy on hard days, and sustainable habits rather than crash diets.

Do you only work with racers?

No. Marine works with women at every level, from leisure riders and sportive cyclists to racers, in Ireland and online worldwide.
