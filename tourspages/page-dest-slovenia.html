Slovenia

NO BETTER PLACE FOR CYCLING

There is no better place than Slovenia for cyclists looking to escape the crowds. Breathtaking alpine scenery, emerald-green rivers and near-empty roads guarantee every rider a sense of complete and utter freedom.

Our tours are based around some of the country's most spectacular terrain, a training ground long favoured by professional cycling teams for its quiet, well-surfaced roads. Here you can cycle through dramatic mountain passes, taste some of Central Europe's best food and wine, and experience a destination most cyclists have never had on their radar.

Our aim is to help make your holiday special, by providing the best routes and the best experiences.

Slovenia is one of the most compact, diverse countries in Europe — in a single day you can ride from alpine peaks to lakeside towns to rolling vineyard hills. Its landscape was shaped by the same glacial forces as its neighbouring Alpine regions, leaving behind dramatic mountain passes in the north and softer, greener terrain further south, all wrapped in one of the most forested countries in Europe.

Slovenia is a destination where discovering the culture and local traditions is effortless at any time of year. The turquoise rivers of the Soča Valley, the fairytale setting of Lake Bled, and the legendary hairpins of the Vršič Pass sit alongside historic towns virtually untouched by mass tourism. Given the clarity of its alpine rivers and the quiet of its mountain roads, Slovenia is a destination where visitors can enjoy a holiday full of relaxation, sport, and the thrill of discovering a country many riders never expected to fall in love with.

When you think of Central European cuisine, Slovenia rarely gets the credit it deserves — but it should. Slovenian food draws on Italian, Austrian, Hungarian and Balkan influences, resulting in a genuinely varied table: potica (a rolled nut pastry), štruklji (rolled dumplings), hearty mountain stews, and excellent charcuterie. The wine regions of the Vipava Valley and Štajerska produce some of Central Europe's most underrated wines. There are countless taverns and restaurants throughout the country, set in wonderful locations among the mountains and valleys, and we will bring you to the best.

Good to know

SLOVENIA FACTS

Location

Slovenia sits at the crossroads of the Alps, the Mediterranean and the Balkans, bordering Italy, Austria, Hungary and Croatia. Despite its small size, it offers an extraordinary range of terrain — from the Julian Alps in the northwest to a short stretch of Adriatic coastline in the southwest.

Climate

Slovenia's climate varies with its terrain: alpine conditions in the mountains, a continental climate across the central plateau and Ljubljana basin, and a milder sub-Mediterranean climate near the coast. Summer temperatures in the lowlands typically range between 22° and 30°C, with cooler conditions at altitude in the Julian Alps.

Cuisine

Slovenian cuisine is one of Europe's best-kept secrets, blending Italian, Austrian, Hungarian and Balkan influences. Beyond potica and štruklji, look out for excellent Vipava Valley wines, fresh trout from alpine rivers, and cured meats from the mountain regions — all reflecting Slovenia's position at the meeting point of several culinary traditions.

Terrain

From the legendary 24 hairpins of the Vršič Pass to the turquoise gravel-lined banks of the Soča Valley and the gentle shores of Lake Bled, Slovenia offers an outstanding mix of high-mountain climbing and quieter valley riding. Roads are well-surfaced and remarkably free of traffic, even on the country's most celebrated climbs.
