Corsica

A CYCLING PARADISE

Corsica is one of the most spectacular cycling destinations in Europe, where towering mountains meet the crystal-clear Mediterranean. Ride quiet coastal roads that climb from turquoise bays into dramatic granite peaks, offering unforgettable scenery and a true sense of freedom. Our tours take you across the island's most iconic routes, including roads featured in the Tour de France. Discover legendary climbs, authentic Corsican cuisine, and breathtaking landscapes that have earned the island its nickname, "L'Île de Beauté" – the Isle of Beauty. Our goal is simple: to create an unforgettable cycling holiday with exceptional routes, local experiences, and outstanding support.

Corsica's cycling appeal comes from its sheer verticality — nowhere else in the Mediterranean do mountains rise so abruptly from the sea. In the space of a single ride you can leave the coastline behind and find yourself deep in granite peaks and chestnut forests, on roads so quiet and dramatic that the Tour de France chose them for its 100th edition Grand Départ.

Corsica is a French island in the Mediterranean, closer to Italy than to mainland France, and its culture reflects that unique position. The legendary Col de Bavella, with its distinctive granite needles, sits alongside dramatic coastal roads and the wild, mountainous interior that inspired the island's famous GR20 hiking trail. Given its combination of alpine climbing and Mediterranean coastline, Corsica is a destination where visitors can enjoy a holiday full of serious riding, striking scenery and the thrill of discovering one of Europe's most underrated cycling islands.

When you think of Corsican food, expect a distinct island identity shaped by its mountains as much as its coastline. Charcuterie is central to the culture — coppa, lonzu and figatellu are all Corsican specialities — alongside brocciu, the island's beloved fresh sheep's cheese, and dishes built around chestnut flour, a legacy of the island's mountain villages. Corsican wines, increasingly recognised beyond the island itself, pair perfectly with a day spent in the saddle. There are countless auberges throughout the island, set in wonderful coastal and mountain locations, and we will bring you to the best.

Good to know

CORSICA FACTS

Location

Corsica sits in the Mediterranean between mainland France and Italy, closer to the Italian coast than to France. Despite its relatively small size, it packs in over 1,000km of coastline and mountains rising to over 2,700m, making it one of the most vertically dramatic islands in Europe.

Climate

Corsica has a Mediterranean climate along its coast, with hot, dry summers around 28-30°C, while the mountainous interior stays noticeably cooler and can retain snow on higher peaks well into late spring — a genuine contrast within a single small island.

Cuisine

Corsican cuisine blends French and Italian influences with a strong identity of its own. Expect exceptional charcuterie (coppa, lonzu, figatellu), brocciu cheese, chestnut-based dishes from the mountain villages, and increasingly well-regarded Corsican wines.

Terrain

Corsica offers some of the most dramatic climbing in the Mediterranean, with roads rising directly from the coastline into granite peaks — the Col de Bavella being the island's most iconic. Expect quiet, well-surfaced mountain roads and coastal routes with stunning sea views throughout.
