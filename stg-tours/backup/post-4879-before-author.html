Cyprus doesn't come up often in cycling conversations, which is exactly why I love guiding here — you get Mediterranean roads and genuine mountain terrain, almost entirely to yourself, in a climate that works for riding nearly year-round in a way few other European destinations can match.

Why Cyprus Works So Well for Winter Cycling

While most of Europe's classic cycling regions shut down for winter, Cyprus's position at the eastern edge of the Mediterranean gives it a genuinely mild climate through the cooler months — this is part of why it's become a quiet favorite among professional teams for early-season training camps, alongside the more famous Mallorca and Tenerife options.

The Troodos Mountains

This is the heart of Cyprus cycling — a range of forested climbs reaching over 1,900m at Mount Olympus (the island's highest point, not to be confused with the more famous Greek mountain of the same name), with a mix of quiet paved roads and well-maintained gravel forestry tracks winding through Aleppo pine and cedar forest. Gradients are moderate but sustained, and the pine forest cover makes for a welcome break from the more exposed, sun-baked coastal roads.

The forestry tracks around Troodos are genuinely excellent gravel riding — wide enough to feel safe and predictable, well-drained thanks to the mountain's rocky base, and largely free of the technical obstacles you'd find on true singletrack. This is approachable gravel riding for anyone comfortable on a road bike, not a mountain-biking test.

Coastal Roads

The southern coast around Limassol and Paphos offers flatter, faster riding with sea views most of the way — a good complement to the mountain days, and ideal for an easier recovery ride between harder Troodos outings. The Akamas Peninsula in the west offers a wilder, less developed coastal option for those wanting a quieter alternative to the more built-up southern coast.

Best Time to Ride

October through April is the sweet spot. Cyprus is genuinely one of the best winter cycling destinations in Europe — daytime temperatures typically sit in the mid-teens to low 20s°C even in January, while northern Europe is under snow and most Alpine and Pyrenean roads are closed entirely. Summer (June–August) is better avoided for serious climbing days; the heat in the lowlands is intense, regularly exceeding 35°C, though the higher elevation of Troodos does offer some relief even in midsummer.

Bike Choice

A gravel bike with 35mm+ tyres opens up the forestry tracks in Troodos that a road bike simply can't access comfortably, while still being entirely comfortable on the coastal tarmac sections. If your trip is coastal-only, a standard road bike is perfectly fine, but you'd be missing what makes Cyprus genuinely distinctive as a cycling destination.

What to Pack

Layering matters more in Cyprus than the "sunny Mediterranean island" reputation suggests — Troodos at 1,900m in winter can be noticeably cold, occasionally with light snow at the very highest points, while the coast the same day might be a mild 18°C. A packable layer for the mountain days, alongside standard warm-weather kit for the coast, covers the full range you'll encounter.

Common Mistakes First-Timers Make

Underestimating the Troodos climbs is the most common one — because Cyprus has a reputation as a beach destination, riders sometimes arrive expecting flat, easy riding and are surprised by the genuine mountain terrain in the interior. Treat the Troodos days with the same respect you'd give an Alpine climbing day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cyprus a good destination for a winter training camp?
Yes — this is exactly why professional teams have used it, alongside Mallorca and Tenerife, as an early-season base. Mild temperatures and genuine climbing terrain make it well suited to structured winter training.

Do I need a gravel bike, or is a road bike enough?
A road bike handles the coastal riding well, but a gravel bike is recommended to access the full range of Troodos and enjoy a full gravel tour with forestry tracks, which are a highlight of the region.

How does the climbing in Troodos compare to the Alps or Pyrenees?
Shorter and generally less steep than the Alps' signature passes, but genuinely substantial climbing nonetheless, particularly cumulative elevation gain over a full riding day.

Is Cyprus cycling suitable for beginners?
The coastal routes suit a wide range of fitness levels well; the Troodos mountain days are better suited to riders with at least moderate climbing experience.
