One of the most common messages I get before someone books is some version of "am I fit enough for this?" So here's a straightforward way to think about it, without the guesswork, built from years of matching riders of every level to the right trip.

Why This Question Matters More Than People Realize

A mismatched fitness level is, in my experience, the single biggest thing that can spoil an otherwise well-run trip. Not because anyone gets left behind — our support structure prevents that — but because a rider who's constantly at their absolute limit doesn't get to enjoy the scenery, the food, or the camaraderie of a group tour the way someone riding within their capability does. Getting this right upfront matters more than most people assume when booking.

Leisure / Moderate Fitness

If you ride a few times a month, can comfortably manage 40-60km with some rolling hills, and aren't looking to push your limits on holiday, the Algarve and Croatia coastal tours are excellent fits. Rolling terrain, stunning scenery, and daily distances that leave plenty of energy for the food and culture side of the trip rather than leaving you depleted by dinner.

These tours suit riders who cycle recreationally, perhaps commute occasionally, or simply want an active holiday without the specific demands of high-mountain climbing.

Solid Recreational Rider

If you ride regularly, are comfortable with 60-90km days and can handle sustained climbs without needing to walk any section, Lake Garda, Lake Como, and Tuscany are well suited — real climbing, but with enough variety in daily profile that no single day is overwhelming, and rest days or shorter options built into the itinerary.

This category covers the majority of our guests — people who ride consistently, perhaps do the occasional organized sportive or gran fondo at home, and want a genuine challenge without needing to be at peak competitive fitness.

Strong, Experienced Climber

If you actively train for climbing, ride 100km+ days at home, and want to genuinely test yourself against legendary gradients, the Dolomites, French Alps, and Pyrenees tours are built for you — these are the tours where the climbs are the main event, not a side feature, and the itinerary is structured around tackling genuinely demanding Tour de France and Giro d'Italia climbs.

What About Gravel-Specific Fitness Demands?

Our Cyprus and Tuscany gravel tours have a slightly different fitness profile — the terrain itself is less about pure climbing gradient and more about sustained effort over rolling, mixed-surface terrain. A rider with solid general endurance but less specific mountain-climbing experience often finds these tours a great fit.

A Few Honest Notes

  • Every tour includes a support vehicle, so no one is ever left to struggle alone on a climb — that safety net exists regardless of fitness level, and it fundamentally changes the risk calculation compared to an unsupported ride
  • Groups are small and paced flexibly, so a mixed-ability group can genuinely still ride together for most of a route, splitting only where the terrain specifically calls for it
  • If you're between categories, it's always worth messaging us directly — matching people to the right trip properly is something we take seriously, since getting this right is one of the biggest factors in whether a guest has a great week or a merely good one

How Age Factors Into This (Or Doesn't)

I want to be clear that this framework is about current fitness and riding habits, not age. We've guided guests well into their 70s on our harder mountain tours, and equally, some younger riders find our "moderate" tours plenty challenging if they're newer to consistent cycling. Recent, consistent riding habit matters far more than age as a predictor of how a guest will handle a given tour.

How We Can Help You Decide

If you're still unsure, tell us your typical weekly riding volume, your longest recent ride, and how comfortable you are on sustained climbs, and we'll point you to the right tour — it takes two minutes and saves a lot of second-guessing, both before booking and on the road itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my fitness doesn't clearly fit one category?
Most riders sit somewhere between categories, which is completely normal — message us with specifics and we'll recommend the best fit, sometimes suggesting a tour one level below what you might initially assume so you genuinely enjoy the week rather than just surviving it.

Can I upgrade my fitness closer to departure if I'm borderline for a harder tour?
Yes, and our preparation guide outlines a structured approach — a borderline rider who commits to consistent training in the 8-12 weeks before departure can genuinely move up a category.

Do harder tours require you to keep pace with a fast group?
No — group pace is set to be inclusive, and support vehicle backup means slower riders are never left stranded, though the terrain itself on our hardest tours is objectively more demanding regardless of pace.

Is it possible to do a custom trip that mixes difficulty levels for a group with mixed fitness?
Yes, this is one of the more common requests for our custom tours, and we regularly design itineraries that let a mixed-ability group travel together while offering optional harder or easier routes on specific days.
