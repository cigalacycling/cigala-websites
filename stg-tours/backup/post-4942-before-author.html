Mallorca has been the training-camp island of choice for professional cycling teams for decades, and once you've ridden it, the reason is obvious — a genuinely rare combination of reliable early-season weather, excellent road surfaces, and a range of terrain from flat coastal spins to serious mountain climbing, all within a compact island you can cross in a day.

Why Pro Teams Choose Mallorca

Every winter and early spring, the island fills with professional teams doing their base training, drawn by mild Mediterranean temperatures when much of continental Europe is still cold, combined with genuinely good climbing terrain in the Serra de Tramuntana mountain range along the northwest coast. This isn't a marketing angle — it's a well-established fact of the professional cycling calendar, and riding the same roads as WorldTour teams in February is part of Mallorca's appeal.

Sa Calobra: The Signature Climb

No Mallorca guide is complete without Sa Calobra, one of the most distinctive climbs in Europe — around 10km with roughly 26 numbered hairpins, including the famous 270-degree "tie knot" loop partway up where the road literally passes back under itself. It's not the longest or steepest climb on the island, but it's unforgettable, dropping from the Tramuntana ridge down to a small cove on the coast, meaning most riders climb it in the "wrong" direction from the sea back up to the main road.

Puig Major

The island's highest paved climb, Puig Major offers a longer, more sustained test than Sa Calobra, climbing through the same Tramuntana range with genuinely mountainous scenery despite the island setting — a reminder that Mallorca's climbing is a legitimate mountain-cycling experience, not just a beach-holiday add-on.

The Rest of the Island

Beyond the Tramuntana climbs, Mallorca offers flat, fast coastal roads perfect for easier days or recovery rides, plus a network of quiet inland roads through almond and olive groves in the island's agricultural heartland — genuine variety within a single, compact destination.

Best Time to Ride

October through May is the prime cycling season, precisely because it avoids the intense summer heat and tourist crowds of the beach season — this inverse seasonality compared to most of our Mediterranean destinations is part of what makes Mallorca such a valuable addition to a touring calendar, filling exactly the months when Alpine and Pyrenean high passes are closed.

Bike Choice

A road bike is the right tool for essentially the entire island — Mallorca's cycling infrastructure is built around road riding, with excellent tarmac throughout, and there's little reason to bring a gravel setup here unless specifically seeking out the island's more remote agricultural back roads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mallorca only good for winter training, or is it worth visiting in other seasons?
Winter and spring are the standout seasons given the mild climate and quiet roads; summer is rideable but hot and considerably busier with general tourism.

How does Sa Calobra compare in difficulty to Alpine climbs like Alpe d'Huez?
Shorter and less demanding overall, though the tie-knot hairpin and consistently technical descent-then-climb structure make it a genuinely memorable, moderately challenging ride rather than an easy spin.

Do professional teams actually train on the same roads tourists ride?
Yes — this is one of the more unusual aspects of Mallorca cycling; the Tramuntana roads used by WorldTour teams for winter training camps are entirely open public roads.

Is Mallorca suitable for a mixed-ability group?
Very much so — the combination of serious mountain climbing and easy flat coastal roads within a compact island makes it one of our more flexible destinations for groups with a range of fitness levels.
