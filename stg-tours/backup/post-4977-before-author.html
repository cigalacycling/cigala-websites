If Tuscany and the Dolomites represent Italy's rolling hills and high mountains, Puglia — the country's southern "heel" — offers something entirely different: predominantly flat to gently rolling terrain, ancient olive groves stretching to the horizon, and a distinctly different, sun-baked Mediterranean character from northern and central Italy.

What the Terrain Is Actually Like

This is genuinely some of the flattest riding terrain in our entire Italian portfolio — long, straight roads through olive groves that in places are centuries old, interrupted occasionally by the whitewashed hill towns and coastal stretches that punctuate the otherwise gentle landscape. It's a refreshing contrast for riders who've done our more mountainous Italian tours and want an easier-paced, high-mileage style of trip instead.

The Trulli Country

The Valle d'Itria region is home to Puglia's famous trulli — distinctive whitewashed conical-roofed stone huts unique to this part of Italy, clustered particularly around the town of Alberobello. Riding through this landscape, past centuries-old olive trees and these unusual traditional structures, gives Puglia a visual character genuinely unlike anywhere else in the country.

The Coastline

Puglia has two coasts — the Adriatic to the east and the Ionian to the west — both offering scenic coastal riding, with the Adriatic side generally better known for its dramatic cliffs and clear water, particularly around the Gargano promontory in the north of the region.

Best Time to Ride

Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) are ideal, avoiding the intense summer heat that makes flat, shade-free Puglian roads considerably less comfortable in July and August. The region's southern latitude means summer heat here can be more punishing than in northern or central Italy.

Food and Culture

Puglia has developed a genuine culinary reputation in its own right — excellent olive oil (the region produces a significant share of Italy's total production), orecchiette pasta, and burrata cheese, all best enjoyed at a relaxed pace after an easier riding day than our mountain-focused tours demand.

Why This Appeals to a Different Kind of Rider

Puglia suits riders prioritizing distance, scenery, and food over serious climbing — a genuinely different holiday style from our Alpine or Pyrenean tours, better compared to Croatia or the Algarve in terms of overall demand, though with its own distinct flat, olive-grove character.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Puglia suitable for beginner cyclists?
Yes, very much so — the flat to gently rolling terrain here is among the most approachable in our entire portfolio, well suited to riders newer to multi-day touring.

How does Puglia compare to Tuscany?
Tuscany offers rolling hills and, on our gravel tour, unpaved sectors; Puglia is flatter and features distinctive trulli architecture and olive grove landscapes rather than Tuscany's vineyard hill country.

What's the cycling distance like on a typical Puglia day?
Generally longer daily distances than our mountain tours, given the flatter terrain — riders often cover more ground per day here with less overall fatigue than an equivalent mountain day would produce.

Is Puglia a good destination for combining cycling with food-focused travel?
Very much so — the region's culinary reputation is a genuine highlight, and the easier riding pace leaves more energy and appetite for the food and wine along the way.
