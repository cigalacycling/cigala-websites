What Is the Tuscany Trail?

The Tuscany Trail is one of Europe's best-known gravel bikepacking routes. Stretching for more than 500 km across Tuscany, it links the northern coast with southern Tuscany using gravel roads, forest tracks, farm lanes, and quiet secondary roads.

Every spring, thousands of cyclists take part in the unsupported Tuscany Trail event, carrying all of their own equipment and completing the route at their own pace. Outside the event, the Tuscany Trail remains one of Italy's most popular long-distance gravel adventures for riders looking to explore Tuscany beyond its famous tourist destinations.

Rather than following a single famous road, the Tuscany Trail connects many of Tuscany's most spectacular landscapes, from the Apuan Alps and Chianti to the Val d'Orcia and the Maremma.

What the Terrain Is Like

One of the reasons the Tuscany Trail has become so popular is the incredible variety of riding.

During the week you'll encounter:

  • Smooth Strade Bianche white gravel roads
  • Rolling vineyard tracks
  • Fast gravel farm roads
  • Forest double-track
  • Quiet tarmac linking remote villages
  • Occasional rougher gravel sections through woodland on the via Francigena

Compared with the classic Strade Bianche sectors around Siena, the terrain is considerably more varied, more gravel, making every day feel different.

Regions You'll Ride Through

The Apuan Alps

The northern mountains of Tuscany offer forest roads, panoramic climbing, and spectacular views towards the famous marble quarries around Carrara.

Chianti

Rolling vineyards, cypress-lined roads, medieval villages, and some of Italy's most famous wine-producing landscapes.

Val d'Orcia

Perhaps Tuscany's most iconic scenery, with endless rolling hills, quiet gravel roads, and UNESCO World Heritage landscapes.

The Maremma

Southern Tuscany has a completely different feel. Wild, rugged, and far quieter than the famous wine regions, it's one of Italy's hidden gems for gravel riding.

Bike Setup

Unlike riding only the Strade Bianche roads, this tour covers a wider range of surfaces.

We recommend:

  • Gravel bike
  • 40–45 mm tyres
  • Low climbing gears
  • Tubeless setup where possible

A modern gravel bike provides the comfort and confidence needed for the more technical forest sections while remaining fast on Tuscany's smoother gravel roads.

Best Time to Ride

Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) offer the best cycling conditions.

These months bring:

  • Mild temperatures
  • Green countryside
  • Quiet roads
  • Ideal gravel conditions

Summer can become extremely hot, particularly through the exposed landscapes of southern Tuscany.

Common Mistakes First-Time Riders Make

Many riders assume the Tuscany Trail is simply an extended version of the Strade Bianche roads.

In reality, it's far more varied.

The biggest mistakes include:

  • Bringing tyres that are too narrow
  • Underestimating the climbing
  • Expecting every gravel road to be smooth white limestone
  • Carrying unnecessary equipment

Our guided tour removes these concerns by providing mechanical support, luggage transfers, and experienced local guides throughout the week.

No. Our Tuscany Gravel Tour is not the official Tuscany Trail event or route. It is an independent, fully supported cycling holiday inspired by some of the most spectacular gravel roads found along the Tuscany Trail, combined with our own carefully selected routes.

Do I need bikepacking experience?

No.

Unlike the unsupported Tuscany Trail event, our tour includes hotels, luggage transfers, support vehicles, and ride leaders, so no camping or bikepacking experience is required.

Is the terrain harder than the Strade Bianche roads?

Some sections are.

While you'll ride the famous white gravel roads around Siena, you'll also encounter rougher forest tracks and more technical and remote gravel roads that require a capable gravel bike.

No — the Strade Bianche sectors are a specific, smaller cluster of white gravel roads near Siena. Our Tuscany Gravel Tour draws on the much longer Tuscany Trail, covering considerably more of the region including areas the Strade Bianche route doesn't touch.

Yes.
