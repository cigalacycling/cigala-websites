Why Work With a Cycling Coach?

A good cycling coach does more than give you workouts.

They provide structure, feedback, and long-term progression.

A coach helps you:

  • Improve FTP and aerobic capacity
  • Train consistently without burnout
  • Peak for key events
  • Avoid overtraining and injury
  • Balance training with work and life
  • Progress year after year (not just short-term gains)

Most cyclists don’t fail because they don’t train enough — they fail because they don’t train systematically.

What Makes a Good Cycling Coach in Ireland?

Not all coaches are equal. Here are the key factors to consider.

1. Experience

Look for a coach with:

  • Real racing experience
  • Coaching experience across different levels
  • A strong understanding of endurance physiology and long-term athlete development
  • Understanding & experience of the key cycling events, terrain, and climbing demands within Ireland and internationally, and what is required to perform in these environments.

A coach who has worked with both beginners and competitive cyclists is usually more adaptable, as they understand how to scale training appropriately across different abilities and goals.

2. Education and Methodology

Good coaching should be based on science, not guesswork.

Modern coaching often includes:

  • Power-based training
  • Critical Power or FTP analysis
  • W’ Prime modelling (anaerobic capacity tracking)
  • Structured training phases and periodisation
  • Fatigue and recovery management
  • Strength training to support performance
  • Mobility work to improve positioning, efficiency, and injury prevention
  • Basic nutrition support to optimise training adaptation and recovery, with access to an in-house sports nutritionist for 1:1 coaching coordinated through your daily plan.

A strong coach should be able to explain why you are doing a workout.

3. Personalisation

Avoid off-the-shelves training plans.

A proper coach should adapt training based on:

  • Your available training time
  • Stress and fatigue
  • Work/family life
  • Equipment (power meter, heart rate, etc.)
  • Goals and event calendar

If your plan never changes, it’s not really coaching.

Coaching is a two-way process. A coach provides structure, guidance, and feedback, while the athlete provides consistent data, communication, and honesty about how training is going.

This includes:

  • Daily wellness check-ins
  • Session feedback
  • RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)
  • Training data review

Without feedback from both sides, coaching becomes guesswork rather than a structured performance process.

4. Communication

One of the biggest differences between good and great coaching is communication.

How quickly are training adjustments made?

How often is feedback provided?

Is there direct access to the coach when needed (e.g. messaging or scheduled calls)?

Clear and consistent communication ensures that training can be adapted in real time when life, fatigue, or performance changes.

Online Cycling Coaching in Ireland

Most coaching today is done online — and for good reason.

With platforms like TrainingPeaks or Vekta, coaches can analyse your training in detail and adjust your plan in real time.

Benefits of online coaching:

  • Works anywhere in Ireland or abroad
  • More detailed data analysis than in-person coaching
  • Flexible communication
  • Better value than traditional coaching

For most cyclists, online coaching is actually more effective than in-person coaching.

Do You Need a Power Meter?

Not essential — but highly recommended.

A power meter allows your coach to track:

  • Training load
  • Fitness trends
  • Fatigue and recovery
  • Performance improvements

However, heart rate-based coaching can still work well, especially for beginners.

How Much Does a Cycling Coach Cost in Ireland?

Prices vary depending on the level of support.

Typically:

  • Basic Training Plans (Limited Support): €30–€80/month
    Structured training plans designed for self-guided athletes who want direction and consistency, with minimal coach interaction.
  • Standard Coaching: €100–€200/month
    Fully personalised coaching with regular plan updates, performance feedback, and ongoing communication.
  • High-Performance Coaching: €200–€400+/month
    Comprehensive 1:1 coaching with daily detailed performance analysis, frequent adjustments, race preparation, and close coach-athlete communication.

The value of coaching is not defined by price, but by the level of personalisation, feedback, and ongoing support you receive.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Coach

Before choosing a cycling coach, ask:

  • Do you personalise training plans?
  • How often are plans updated?
  • What platform do you use?
  • Can training adapt if my schedule changes?
  • Do you provide race analysis?
  • How do you measure progress?
  • How often can we talk?

A good coach will answer all of these clearly.
